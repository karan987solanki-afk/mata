About the Temple

Where is Salkanpur Temple?

Salkanpur Temple, also called Bijasan Mata Mandir (spelled Beejasan or Vijayasana in some sources), stands on a hilltop in Salkanpur village, Rehti tehsil, Sehore district — around 70 km from Bhopal.

Mythology

The Legend of Bijasan Mata

The temple's story is deeply linked to the fierce battle between Goddess Durga and the demon Raktabeej, whose blood gave rise to a new demon with every drop that touched the ground.

local_florist

"Destroyer of the Seed"

Bijasan Mata — understood to mean "destroyer of the seed" — is associated with ending Raktabeej's endless cycle, honoured as a site where good triumphed over evil.

auto_awesome

Spiritual Power

The deity is believed to be self-manifested (swayambhu) within the sanctum. Local tradition also connects the hill to the wider legend of Shakti Peethas.

Origins

History at a Glance

Circa 1100 AD — Gond Origins

Local tradition holds the original temple structure was built by the Gond people, whose presence shaped much of the Vindhyan region.

Later Centuries — Expansion

Some accounts trace further construction to later periods, including the Mughal era, as the temple's following grew across central India.

2001 — Modern Access

A ropeway was built connecting the base to the hilltop, making the shrine accessible to elderly devotees.

Present Day — Devi Lok

A "Devi Lok" development, inspired by Ujjain's Mahakal Lok, is under construction under the Salkanpur Trust to expand pilgrim facilities.

Beyond the Temple

Places to Visit Around Salkanpur

Most pilgrims combine their Salkanpur Mata darshan with a short trip around Sehore district, home to forests, dams, and heritage sites.

Kolar Dam

Scenic dam with boating and sunset views, on the Bhopal–Salkanpur route.

Bhimbetka Rock Shelters

UNESCO World Heritage prehistoric cave art site (~40 km away), with paintings estimated at thousands of years old.

Ratapani Tiger Reserve

Forest tiger reserve ~30 km away — tigers, leopards, sloth bears, and over 150 recorded bird species.

Bhadbhada & Amargarh Falls

Seasonal waterfalls popular during the monsoon, near Salkanpur.

Ginnourgarh Fort

A 15th-century Gond-era hill fort (~50 km away), popular with heritage trekkers.

Sehore Town

District HQ ~35 km away, with the 1838 All Saints' Church and local markets.

Saru-Maru Caves

Ancient Buddhist monastic site with rock-cut caves and stupas.

Kubereshwar Dham

Well-known Shiva temple in Sehore district, often visited alongside Salkanpur.

Many visitors combine a 2-day trip: Day 1 for Salkanpur darshan, Day 2 for a Ratapani forest visit or Bhimbetka — worth planning ahead given driving distances between sites.

What Does "Beejasan" Mean?

One explanation offered by local sources breaks the name into two parts: Beej, referring to the seed-syllable mantras used in devotional worship, and asan, meaning "seat." Together, Beejasan is understood by some devotees as "the Goddess seated upon sacred mantras" — though, as with much oral temple tradition, exact etymology varies by source.

construction

The Devi Lok Project

A large-scale development, inspired by Ujjain's Mahakal Lok, is underway at Salkanpur under state and temple trust initiatives. Reported plans include an upgraded multi-station ropeway, better pilgrim facilities, and landscaped walkways depicting stories of the Goddess. Specific costs and completion dates vary by report, so treat any figures you see elsewhere with caution until officially confirmed.

Before You Go

Essential Visitor Tips

checkroomWhat to Wear & Carry

  • • Modest, comfortable clothing — many temples in the region discourage shorts and sleeveless tops
  • • Sturdy walking shoes if climbing the stairs (some devotees go barefoot for religious reasons)
  • • A water bottle, especially for the climb
  • • Cash for prasad, parking, and ropeway tickets — card payment isn't guaranteed everywhere
  • • A light jacket in winter months, and sun protection in summer

infoGood to Know

  • • During Navratri, private vehicles may be restricted near the hill — shared jeeps or the ropeway are used instead
  • • Photography may be restricted inside the main sanctum
  • • Monkeys are common near the stairs — keep food secured
  • • Keep valuables secure in crowded periods
  • • For the latest on any of this, ask the community Q&A board — fellow visitors often have the most current info

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Salkanpur?

Salkanpur is a hilltop village in Rehti tehsil, Sehore district, Madhya Pradesh, known for the Salkanpur Mata Mandir — a temple dedicated to Maa Vindhyavasini Bijasan Devi.

What is the distance from Bhopal to Salkanpur?

Approximately 70 km, roughly 1.5–2 hours by road. Check the community Q&A board for current bus timings.

Is Salkanpur Wali Mata Mandir Barkheda Salam the same temple?

No — that's a separate, smaller temple within Bhopal city, also dedicated to Bijasan Devi. This site covers the original hilltop temple in Rehti tehsil, Sehore district.

Is Bijasan Mata the same as Beejasan Devi or Vijayasana Mata?

Yes — Bijasan, Beejasan, and Vijayasana are different spellings of the same name, all referring to Maa Vindhyavasini Devi.
