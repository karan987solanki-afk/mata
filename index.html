Basic Information

About the Temple

Salkanpur Temple, also called Bijasan Mata Mandir, stands on a hilltop in Salkanpur village, Rehti tehsil, in the Sehore district of Madhya Pradesh — around 70 km from Bhopal. The presiding deity, Maa Vindhyavasini Bijasan Devi, is worshipped as a form of Goddess Durga and is regarded as self-manifested (swayambhu) within the sanctum. The temple is considered a Siddhapeeth, a site believed to hold special spiritual power, and draws thousands of pilgrims through the year.

Devotees reach the sanctum by climbing close to a thousand stone steps, or by a ropeway built in 2001 for those who cannot make the climb. From the hilltop, the temple offers wide views over the surrounding forests and hills of the Vindhyachal range. Idols of Lord Bhairav, Goddess Saraswati, and Goddess Lakshmi are also present in the temple courtyard.

Mythology

The Legend

Durga and the Demon Raktabeej

The temple's story is linked to the tale of Goddess Durga's battle with the demon Raktabeej, whose blood gave rise to a new demon with every drop that touched the ground. Bijasan Mata — a name understood to mean "destroyer of the seed" — is associated with ending this cycle, and the site is honoured as a place where good triumphed over evil.

Local tradition also connects the hill to the wider legend of Shakti Peethas, sacred sites said to mark where parts of the Goddess fell to earth. Devotees regard Salkanpur as one such place of deep spiritual significance in the region.

Origins

History at a Glance

Circa 1100 AD

Local tradition holds that the original temple structure was built by the Gond people, whose presence shaped much of the surrounding Vindhyan region.

Later Centuries

Some accounts trace further construction and expansion at the site to later periods, including the Mughal era, as the temple's following grew across central India.

2001

A ropeway was built connecting the base to the hilltop, making the shrine accessible to elderly devotees and those unable to climb the stairs.

Present Day

The temple continues to be maintained and renovated under the Salkanpur Trust, with the "Devi Lok" development — inspired by Ujjain's Mahakal Lok — under construction to expand facilities for pilgrims.

Visiting

How to Reach Salkanpur

By Air

The nearest airport is Raja Bhoj Airport, Bhopal, roughly 70 km away.

By Road

Salkanpur is well connected by road from Bhopal, Sehore, and Hoshangabad. Taxis and buses run regularly along this route.

At the Hill

From the base, pilgrims can either climb the stone stairway to the summit or take the ropeway, which passes over a pond and forest on the way up.

Best Time to Visit

The temple is especially crowded during Navratri, when a large annual fair is also held on the site.

When to Go

Festivals

Navratri is the principal festival at Salkanpur, drawing large numbers of devotees who climb the stairway chanting "Jai Mata Di." A grand fair (mela) is held at the temple grounds during this period, and some devotees observe a vow of visiting on foot and avoiding certain foods during the Navdurga days as an act of devotion.

Beyond the Temple

Places to Visit Around Salkanpur

Most pilgrims combine their Salkanpur Mata darshan with a short trip around Sehore district, which is dotted with forests, waterfalls, dams, and heritage sites. Here are some of the well-known spots near Salkanpur worth adding to your itinerary.

Kolar Dam

A scenic dam on the Kolar River with boating and pleasant sunset views, popular with visitors travelling between Bhopal and Salkanpur.

Bhimbetka Rock Shelters

A UNESCO World Heritage prehistoric rock art site with ancient cave paintings, located on the way between Bhopal and Salkanpur.

Ratapani Wildlife Sanctuary

A forested tiger reserve near Delawadi offering nature drives, birdwatching, and jungle camping close to the Salkanpur route.

Bhadbhada & Amargarh Waterfalls

Seasonal waterfalls in the region that come alive during the monsoon, popular for short nature outings near Salkanpur.

Kairi Mahadeo & Ginnograh Fort

A forest shrine and a nearby hill fort, both accessible from the Delawadi forest area close to Salkanpur.

Sehore Town

The district headquarters, about 35 km from Salkanpur, with the colonial-era All Saints' Church (built 1838), local markets, and the Siwan riverfront.

Saru-Maru Caves

An ancient Buddhist monastic site in Sehore district with early rock-cut caves and stupas, of interest to heritage travellers.

Kubereshwar Dham

A well-known Shiva temple in Sehore district that draws large crowds during religious gatherings, often visited alongside Salkanpur.

Travel tip: since Salkanpur lies directly on the Bhopal–Rehti–Hoshangabad route, many visitors plan a day trip covering Bhopal, Bhimbetka, and Salkanpur together, or extend the trip toward Kolar Dam and the Ratapani forest belt.
